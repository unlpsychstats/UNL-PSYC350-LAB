Welcome!

The purpose of this laboratory is to reinforce the information presented in lecture and to give you a chance to experience and practice many of those procedures and techniques. While the timing and approach to topic coverage will not be perfectly matched between lecture and lab, the content of the two is very close. Topics and techniques covered in successive laboratory meetings are often interrelated and depend on what you learned in the previous laboratory.

The policies here (attendance, punctuality, preparedness, assignment completion, etc.) are designed to help you participate fully, learn as much as possible from each laboratory, and receive credit for what you have learned.

Objectives & Strategies

NoteIn PSYC 350 you will learn about two important skill sets:

  1. the ability to design, complete, interpret, and report empirical research to test hypotheses derived from psychological theory or its application, and

  2. the ability to critically evaluate research produced by others and to propose changes in the design, analysis, or interpretation of it.

Justification

You might not be planning a career as a research psychologist, and many professional psychologists do not themselves contribute to the research literature. However, all psychologists must be competent “consumers” of the psychological research literature in their area of expertise, whether it is clinical, counseling, consulting, educational, law-psych, I/O, human resources, human factors, or some other area of psychology.

Also, most jobs held by professional psychologists require substantial data-analysis and report-writing skills, whether to document decision making, obtain funding, provide evidence for the efficacy of service delivery, or contribute to the professional literature. In addition, the ability to produce competent, theoretically relevant empirical research is usually the “ticket for admission” to a career as a professional psychologist.

Completion and presentation of your own independent research (such as an honors thesis or a presentation or poster at a student research conference) is often helpful evidence that you can “do research” when applying to graduate school. Most Ph.D. programs require at least two empirical research projects—the master’s thesis and the Ph.D. dissertation.

Classroom Climate

We have to be able to talk forthrightly about the topics of this class and to explore the history, current approaches, and likely future of our field. Some sensitive topics are the variables and labels we use to define target populations and describe who is in our samples. In particular, we must be aware of the standards and preferences in our own research or application communities, as well as differences across communities.

Our class welcomes students from multiple colleges and programs, not all of whom use the same descriptive categories or labels for those categories. We will also discuss other sensitive topics, such as researcher motivations and bias. As a group, we must balance comfort and coverage. Please let me know if we are pushing too far toward one or the other. Showing empathy and compassion for each other can help. Listen to one another, be aware of other people’s needs, and give each other grace.

Everyone who attends this class is entitled to feel welcome and safe from intimidation, insult, bullying, harassment, discrimination of any kind, and unwelcome sexual contact or advances. We will consistently treat each other with respect and support. Both agreement and disagreement can drive learning, understanding, and successful application; both are best handled with mutual respect, patience, and grace.

Emphasis

With respect to design issues, we will pay special attention to the “rules of evidence” for the quantitative analysis of cause-and-effect relationships and the important differences between experimental and nonexperimental designs. With respect to data-analysis issues, we will pay particular attention to the family of ordinary least-squares techniques that includes analysis of variance and linear regression. We will emphasize the pragmatics of hypothesis testing, data analysis, and communication of findings at a level that is more like “driver’s education” and less like “mechanical engineering.” As data collectors and analysts, we have a particular obligation to avoid treating data and results from our samples as if they represent everybody.

In particular, we need to avoid generalizing results from majority-dominated samples as if they were universal or sufficient. We must always ask, “From whom were the data sampled?” We will expand the usual meaning of sampling to include not only who, but also where, when, and how.

General Goals of the Laboratory

By the completion of this course students should be able to:

  • Find existing literature on a topic in psychology, choose relevant articles or chapters, and use that literature to write a review that supports a novel research proposal with specific hypotheses.

  • Identify and evaluate the components of a research report or summary, including its purpose and research hypotheses; sampling and participant-assignment procedures; manipulation and measurement procedures; statistical analyses; and interpretations.

  • Given a research question and/or a set of data, select, perform, and communicate the results of the appropriate statistical analysis (including the statistical null hypothesis) in written or oral form.

  • Use a statistical software package to analyze data, recognize the package’s limitations, and determine how and when to supplement it with hand calculations.

  • Communicate the purpose, hypotheses, procedures, data, analysis results, and interpretations of a research project using conventional written formats and styles (e.g., APA-style papers and posters).

  • Become familiar with online resources and other instructional technologies likely to be used in later courses or research.

How PSYC 350 fits into ACE Outcome(s)

SLO10: Generate a creative or scholarly product that requires broad knowledge, appropriate technical proficiency, information collection, synthesis, interpretation, presentation, and reflection. Research Paper: Students will submit a major research paper in which they introduce the topic by connecting their research to reports that have been published in the scientific literature, describe the research methods, report the results including appropriate statistical analyses, and discuss the implications of the findings in light of other research on that topic.

Reinforcements

Writing

Writing will be reinforced by means of several reports submitted as required projects in the laboratory, and by the final research paper.

Mathematics and Statistics

Mathematics and Statistics will be reinforced throughout the semester as students learn to identify appropriate statistical tests to be used in specific research situations. Students will learn some of the mathematical groundwork for statistical tests, but more importantly they will learn the pros and cons of various tests and whether their assumptions are met in certain applications. Students will learn to compute statistical tests using common statistical software packages, and how to interpret the results.

Critical Thinking

Critical thinking will be reinforced by reading research reports to identify the authors’ research designs, evaluate the validity of their measurements, and critique the appropriateness of their conclusions in light of the data.

Laboratory Schedule, Topics, and Assignments Responsibility Policy

The topics, materials, and assignments for each laboratory meeting are provided in the laboratory and lecture Canvas courses. You are responsible for all material and assignments for each meeting, including changes announced in lecture, in laboratory, or by email.

Laboratory Attendance Policy

You must complete the laboratory section to receive credit for PSYC 350. You are required to attend all portions of each meeting of the laboratory section in which you enrolled.

You may not switch laboratory sections; attend the section in which you enrolled.

Inclement Weather Policy

See the UNL Inclement Weather policy. If in-person classes are canceled, you will receive the instructional continuity plan through Canvas, including instructions for proceeding with the laboratory.

Laboratory Late Attendance Policy

Part of each laboratory meeting will likely involve the TA lecturing about and/or demonstrating topics and techniques. Many laboratory meetings involve students working together on in-laboratory exercises and assignments. You are expected to be on time for all laboratory meetings. If you have to be late, please contact your TA ahead of time.

Laboratory Materials Policy

The instructional and assignment materials for each laboratory meeting are available in your Canvas Lab Course. Having them with you is important for your participation in the laboratory meeting. You are expected to have all the materials for that laboratory meeting with you at the beginning of each laboratory meeting, either as paper copies or, more likely, as digital copies that you can access.

In-Laboratory & Take-Home Laboratory Assignment Policy & Late Penalties

You should assume that all laboratory assignments are due by the laboratory meeting after they are assigned. You are expected to complete all the portions of each assignment and to submit the completed assignment electronically by the deadline set by the TA. If you are not sure when an assignment is due, ask your TA. Assignments turned in before the submission deadline will not be graded upon submission. Grading will not be begun before the submission date & time for each assignment.

An assignment submitted electronically after the deadline will lose up to 10% of its value and an additional 10% for each weekday it is late. For example, consider an assignment due Wednesday at 11:00 a.m.:

  1. An assignment submitted before 11:00 a.m. is on time.
  2. An assignment submitted after 11:00 a.m. on Wednesday is late and would lose 10%.
  3. An assignment submitted on Thursday would lose 20%.
  4. An assignment submitted on Monday would lose 40% (the late penalty applies only to weekdays).

In-Laboratory and Take-Home Assignment Grading: Incomplete Assignments

Only completed assignments will be graded. If you leave parts of an assignment blank or incomplete, your TA will not grade any of the assignment and will return the assignment to you with a grade of “0”. You should complete the assignment and submit it electronically. Late penalties apply for assignments returned because they were incomplete.

Feedback on Graded In-Laboratory and Take-Home Assignments

Feedback on graded assignments will primarily identify errors or mistakes. The TA will not provide the correct answers. You are expected to consult the related handouts, identify the correct answer, and rewrite your assignment. If you cannot resolve an issue from the handouts, email your TA or meet with them during office hours for specific help. Your TA still will not give you the answer, but will direct you to relevant material and help you reason through it. The rewrite option is an opportunity for you to learn and apply material that you either did not learn the first time or did not apply correctly. The goal is to use information from class meetings, laboratory meetings, and handouts to improve your work and your grade. The TA facilitates your use and understanding of the material, but you must do the learning and the work.

Rewrites of Graded In-Laboratory and Take-Home Assignments

Any assignment may be rewritten, and the higher score will count toward your laboratory grade. Revisions of take-home assignments are due at the laboratory meeting after the graded assignment is returned and will not be accepted after that meeting.

Two important rules:

  • The late penalty for rewrites is the same as for the original assignment. A rewrite submitted after the deadline will lose 10% of the assignment’s value and an additional 10% for each weekday it is late.

  • Points lost for a late submission cannot be recovered by rewriting the assignment. For example, if an assignment submitted one day late earned 80%, the 20% late penalty would produce a grade of 60%. If you rewrote the assignment and submitted the rewrite on time, the highest possible grade would be 80% (100% for perfect work minus the original 20% late penalty).

Do the best work you can on each assignment and submit it on time. Use the rewrite option to correct material you misunderstood or misapplied, not as a strategy for recovering from deliberately incomplete work. Incomplete assignments will be returned before grading and will often become late. TAs will not provide the answers to missed items when grading, by email, or during office hours. Time will also pass before you can revise the assignment, while new work continues to be assigned.

Laboratory Assignments and Laboratory Grade Policy

No single laboratory assignment contributes a large share of your overall PSYC 350 grade. During busy periods, you might be tempted to skip an assignment and accept a zero. However, each laboratory assignment is important: You cannot receive a passing grade in this class unless you complete all take-home laboratory assignments.

Academic Integrity and Working Together

Academic integrity is a fundamental value of the University community. Read this syllabus and each assignment’s instructions carefully. You are expected to do your own work, be honest in the statements you make, cite the words and ideas of others, refrain from improperly helping others, and follow the rules for each assignment. If you are unsure whether a form of assistance is permitted, ask the instructor before submitting your work.

Conduct that violates the University of Nebraska Student Code of Conduct will be handled under UNL’s current academic integrity process. Academic misconduct determined to have occurred is reported to Student Conduct & Community Standards. Course-level academic sanctions may be imposed in addition to outcomes from the Student Conduct process, and students retain the due-process and appeal rights described by University policy. This course does not promise an automatic rewrite or a predetermined consequence for a first or subsequent incident.

Some laboratory assignments permit collaboration on a “working” component, such as an exercise or calculation with a single correct answer. When an assignment permits that collaboration, group members may arrive at the same result. Unless the assignment explicitly says otherwise, however, all writing must be your own. Different students are expected to produce different prose.

Individual Needs Accommodation

Students with disabilities are encouraged to contact the instructor for a confidential discussion of their individual needs for academic accommodation. The University of Nebraska-Lincoln provides reasonable accommodations to students with documented disabilities that may affect their participation in course activities or ability to meet course requirements. Students should register with Services for Students with Disabilities (SSD), share their accommodation plan through AIM, and meet with the instructor to discuss how approved accommodations will be implemented in this course.
